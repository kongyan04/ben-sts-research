Proof Quality Assessment

The BP-Free paper contains a complete convergence proof (Sections 2.1–2.4) with three component analyses and a global combination. Here is an honest assessment of its readiness for submission.

What the proof does well. The structure is complete and valid: three independent convergence analyses for θc (client), θa (auxiliary), and θs (server), each using the same L-smoothness + bias-variance decomposition + telescoping template, then combined globally via Cauchy-Schwarz and AM-GM. Theorem 1 is clearly stated with an O(1/√T) dominant rate. The proof recovers standard single-cut split learning convergence exactly when ε → 0, which is a meaningful sanity check. This structure is above the bar for JSR, CJSJ, STS Scholar, and JSHS as a standalone theory paper.
The one remaining gap: ε is asserted, not derived. Assumption 4 states ‖Δa − Δtraditional‖ ≤ ε to bound the auxiliary gradient bias, but ε is never characterized in terms of Ca's architecture or training. A reviewer will ask: under what conditions does the auxiliary network satisfy this bound, and how large is ε in practice? Two valid paths:
  • Path 1 (easier): Define ε explicitly as "the maximum gradient approximation error of Ca relative to a full backward pass, achievable when Ca is sufficiently expressive." This makes the theorem self-contained and is sufficient for JSR, CJSJ, STS Scholar, and JSHS immediately.
  • Path 2 (stronger): Derive ε ≤ f(training loss of Ca, Lipschitz constant Lb of Mb, dimension of the cut-layer activation). This tells practitioners how expressive Ca must be to achieve ε-accuracy — the result that is actually missing from the DSL paper. Unlocks Involve, Davidson, and STS top-40.

The One Action That Unlocks Everything

Define ε — Path 1 (minimum viable, do this week)
Add one sentence before Theorem 1: "Let ε denote the maximum gradient approximation error of the auxiliary network Ca relative to a full backward pass through Mb, i.e., ‖Δa − Δtraditional‖ ≤ ε for all training steps." This makes Assumption 4 self-contained. Unlocks JSR, CJSJ, JSHS, and STS Scholar immediately — no other changes needed.
Derive ε — Path 2 (strongest version, needed for Involve and STS top-40)
Show that \(\varepsilon \leq f(\varepsilon_{\text{aux}},\ L_b,\ \dim(z))\) where εaux is Ca's training loss, Lb is the Lipschitz constant of Mb, and dim(z) is the cut-layer activation size. This tells a practitioner exactly how expressive Ca needs to be to guarantee ε-accuracy. It is the result the DSL paper gestures toward informally ("the client's loss is aligned with the final task") but never formalizes. Ask Dr. Huang whether this is derivable given Ca's architecture — a 30-minute conversation is the highest-value use of time right now.
