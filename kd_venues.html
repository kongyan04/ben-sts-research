Venue Plan

This paper has two distinct submission tracks: the full multi-author paper (Dr. Huang's group + Benjamin) targeting ML conferences, and Benjamin's individual contribution targeting high-school research venues. The strategy differs significantly between the two.

Track 1 — Full Paper (Multi-Author)

arXiv preprint Strong None — post as-is Establishes priority, enables citation. Should be posted regardless of conference submission timeline.

ICML / NeurIPS main track Possible Option A preferred — reviewers will check Appendix A The empirical setting (sequential heterogeneous SL) is novel and the results are strong. Proof needs to match the algorithm before top-venue submission.

Track 2 — Benjamin's Individual Submission

Benjamin is listed as independent contributor (not affiliated with Montclair State). For high-school venues, the framing should emphasize: (1) Benjamin's specific contribution to the convergence analysis, and (2) the practical significance of the forgetting problem in split learning.

VenueFitBest optionNotes
STS — Scholar (top-300) Strong Option B — empirical contribution framed as primary, appendix proof as supporting analysis The forgetting problem is concrete and impactful. +13.7pp improvement is a clear, communicable result. Advisorship under Dr. Huang strengthens the submission. The incomplete proof should be acknowledged.
STS — Finalist (top-40) Possible Option A — fixed proof would differentiate from other ML submissions Most STS finalists are applied projects. A paper with both strong empirics AND a fixed convergence proof would be competitive. Option B alone is unlikely to reach top-40.
JSR Strong Option B sufficient Empirical results are strong enough on their own for JSR. The clearest path: write a 6–8 page JSR version focused on the forgetting problem, experimental setup, and results. Proof in appendix, disclaimed as preliminary.
CJSJ Strong Option B — 3-page limit means experiments only Focus on the setting (sequential non-IID clients), the method (warm-start EMA distillation), and Table I. Drop the proof entirely at this length. Strongest CJSJ submission of the two papers.
JSHS Strong Option B Oral presentation format. The forgetting problem with before/after accuracy comparisons is highly visual and accessible. Judges will respond well to the concrete improvement numbers.
Involve / PUMP Weak Option A only — and requires full proof rewrite Math journals need the proof to be the contribution. With the current appendix, not submittable to Involve. If Option A is completed, could be reframed as a theoretical paper — but the BP-Free paper is a better fit for Involve.
Davidson Fellows Possible Option A preferred Davidson requires a substantial individual contribution. Benjamin's role as independent contributor on a multi-author paper needs careful framing. A fixed proof that Benjamin derives would strengthen the individual contribution claim significantly.
Recommended submission order for Benjamin: (1) arXiv + CJSJ immediately — no proof work needed. (2) JSR within 4–6 weeks — write empirical-focused paper with proof disclaimed. (3) STS with Option B proof cleanup. (4) If time permits, Option A proof fixes for Davidson / STS finalist push.
