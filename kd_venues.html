Proof Quality Assessment

Benjamin completed a full convergence proof for the KD paper (Eq. 14–58). This changes the venue picture significantly. Here is an honest assessment of what the proof achieves and what still needs attention.

What the proof does well. The structure is complete and mathematically valid: L-smoothness descent → 4-component client drift split → Er bound → learning rate constraint → telescoping → final convergence bound. The dominant rate O(1/√KLR) is correct for non-convex federated optimization. The 5-term bound (Eq. 57–58) is detailed and captures both variance (σ²) and data heterogeneity (ζ²) explicitly. This is above the bar for JSR, CJSJ, STS Scholar, and JSHS — as a standalone theory paper.
The one remaining gap: βK is undefined. The term βK appears in the learning rate constraint (Eq. 40: η ≤ 1/(6LKL(1+β²K))) and in the final bound (Eq. 57: LA(1+β²K)/R), but is never defined. A reviewer will immediately ask what βK is. Two valid paths:
  • Path 1 (easier): Define βK explicitly as a data heterogeneity / gradient drift parameter capturing the additional misalignment induced by the KD distillation loss relative to standard supervised gradients. This makes the proof self-contained and gives Theorem 1 a clear interpretation.
  • Path 2 (stronger): Derive a bound βK ≤ f(temperature T, KL divergence between teacher and student logits). This directly connects the proof to the EMA-KD mechanism and is what Involve and top-40 STS judges will want.
Defining βK is a single focused addition that unlocks multiple venues.

The One Action That Unlocks Everything

Define βK (Path 1 — minimum viable)
Add one sentence before Eq. 39: "Let βK denote the gradient drift coefficient induced by the distillation loss relative to standard supervised gradients, satisfying E‖∇fKD(x) − ∇f(x)‖² ≤ β²K‖∇f(x)‖²." This makes Theorem 1 self-contained and unlocks JSR, CJSJ, STS Scholar, and JSHS immediately.
Derive βK (Path 2 — strongest version)
Show that βK ≤ f(T, KL(qT‖pT), α) where T is the distillation temperature and α is the EMA decay rate. This connects the abstract parameter to the algorithm's actual hyperparameters — a practitioner can compute it before training. Unlocks Involve, Davidson, and STS top-40.
