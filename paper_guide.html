Part 2 — Paper Writing Guide

This guide shows how to turn the BP-Free proof into a standalone submission. The same structure applies to the KD paper once βK is defined.

Paper Structure (6–8 pages for JSR; 10–12 for Involve)

Section 1  ·  ~0.5 pages
Introduction

Open with the problem: standard split learning requires a global backward pass, which is incompatible with federated settings where data cannot leave each client. State the gap: no convergence guarantee exists for the two-cut BP-free variant. State the contribution in one sentence: "We prove the first convergence theorem for backpropagation-free split learning with auxiliary networks, establishing an O(1/√T) rate." End with a one-paragraph paper outline.

Key sentence to include: "The convergence analysis for the DSL model with auxiliary networks is explicitly left open in [Zihad et al.] — this paper provides it."

Section 2  ·  ~0.5 pages
Related Work

Three short paragraphs: (1) Split learning and its variants — cite the original SL paper and DSL. (2) Federated learning convergence — cite FedAvg (McMahan et al. 2017) and SCAFFOLD. (3) Auxiliary network approaches in BP-free learning — cite the DSL paper and 1–2 others. The point is to show that convergence for this specific setting has not been proved.

Keep this short for JSR. Involve will want a more thorough review — expand it when writing the Involve version.

Section 3  ·  ~1 page
Problem Setup & Assumptions

Describe the two-cut model: client side Mc, auxiliary network Ca, server side Ms. State all four assumptions formally: (1) L-smoothness for each component, (2) bounded stochastic gradient variance σ², (3) bounded client heterogeneity, (4) auxiliary bias ε — this is where you add the definition of ε. Write it as: "Assumption 4 (Auxiliary Bias). There exists ε ≥ 0 such that at every training step, ‖Δa − Δtraditional‖ ≤ ε, where Δtraditional denotes the gradient signal from a full backward pass through Mb."

Add one sentence after Assumption 4: "In practice, ε decreases as Ca is made more expressive — deriving a formal bound on ε in terms of Ca's architecture is a direction for future work." This honestly scopes the assumption.

Section 4  ·  ~3 pages
Convergence Analysis

Present the proof in four subsections matching Sections 2.1–2.4 of your draft: θc convergence, θa convergence, θs convergence, and the global combination. End with Theorem 1 in a formal theorem box. After the theorem, add a Remark: "When ε → 0, Theorem 1 recovers the standard convergence rate of single-cut split learning, confirming BP-free training as a sound generalization."

For JSR: you can abbreviate Sections 2.2 and 2.3 with "by an analogous argument to Section 2.1." For Involve: write out all three in full — reviewers will check every step.

Section 5  ·  ~0.5 pages
Design Implications

Derive two actionable rules directly from Theorem 1: (1) For ε-accuracy, the auxiliary network must satisfy ε = O(√εtarget) — so a tighter target accuracy demands a more expressive Ca. (2) Once ε² falls below the variance floor σ², improving Ca further yields no benefit — this gives a stopping condition for auxiliary network training. These are the results that make the theorem useful to practitioners.

This section is what separates a math paper from a useful paper. Even for JSR, include it — it shows you understand why the theorem matters.

Section 6  ·  ~1 page (optional for JSR, recommended for Involve)
Experiments

Two experiments are sufficient: (1) MNIST — show convergence curves for varying ε (approximate by controlling Ca capacity). Verify the O(1/√T) rate visually. (2) UCI Heart Disease (4 real hospital institutions) — show that BP-free federated training achieves competitive accuracy relative to standard split learning. The goal is to show the theorem predicts real behavior, not to maximize accuracy.

For JSR submission: leave this section as "Experiments (preliminary)" with one figure if available. Rolling deadline means you can submit now and strengthen later.

Section 7  ·  ~0.5 pages
Conclusion

Restate the contribution in 2–3 sentences. Identify two concrete open problems: (1) deriving a formal bound on ε from Ca's architecture (Path 2 on the BP Venues page), and (2) extending the analysis to the sequential client setting of the KD paper. The second open problem connects the two papers and sets up your future work.

Key Sentences to Write Before Anything Else

These four sentences unlock the paper. Write them first — everything else fills in around them.
  1. The gap sentence (Introduction): "The convergence of backpropagation-free split learning with auxiliary networks has not been formally analyzed."
  2. The contribution sentence (Introduction): "We prove an O(1/√T) convergence bound for BP-free split learning, establishing it as a mathematically rigorous alternative to standard split learning in federated settings."
  3. The ε definition (Assumption 4): "There exists ε ≥ 0 such that ‖Δa − Δtraditional‖ ≤ ε at every training step."
  4. The recovery remark (after Theorem 1): "When ε → 0, the bound recovers the convergence rate of standard single-cut split learning."
