What Is Rigorous: Sections 2.1–2.3

The three per-component convergence proofs follow the standard nonconvex SGD template (cf. Ghadimi & Lan 2013) correctly. Each section:

  1. Applies the L-smoothness descent lemma
  2. Substitutes the gradient update rule \(\theta^{K+1} = \theta^K - \eta \nabla F(\theta^K; \xi^K)\)
  3. Takes expectation over the stochastic gradient
  4. Applies bias-variance decomposition: \(\mathbb{E}[\|X\|^2] = \|\mathbb{E}[X]\|^2 + \mathbb{E}[\|X - \mathbb{E}[X]\|^2]\)
  5. Telescopes the sum and rearranges to get \(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{K=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla F^K\|^2] \leq \sigma^2 + \frac{2L(F^0 - F^*)}{T}\)

A reviewer would accept these steps.

The Problems: Section 2.4

1. Component convergences are orphaned

Sections 2.1–2.3 prove \(F_b\), \(F_a\), and \(F_t\) each converge individually. Then Section 2.4 completely ignores those results and starts over with a new global descent inequality. Equations (19), (29), and (39) are never cited or used in Section 2.4. A reviewer will immediately ask: what do those three subsections contribute?

2. Equation (40) — the key substitution is unjustified

Eq. (40) — problematic
\[F^{K+1} \leq F^K - \eta(\nabla F_a(\theta_a))^T \nabla F^K + \frac{L_\text{overall}}{2}\| {-\eta \nabla F_a}\|^2\]

This substitutes \(\nabla F_a\) (the auxiliary network gradient) for the true gradient \(\nabla F\) in the L-smoothness descent lemma. The justification for this is the auxiliary bias assumption (eq. 7), which says \(\|\mathbb{E}[\nabla F_a] - \nabla_{\theta_b} F\| \leq \varepsilon\). But eq. 7 only bounds the error against \(\nabla_{\theta_b} F\) — the client-side component of the gradient — not the full model gradient \(\nabla F\). The jump from eq. 7 to using \(\nabla F_a\) as a proxy for the global update in eq. (40) is the single most important unjustified step in the proof. This is the load-bearing step the rest of Section 2.4 depends on.

3. Eq. (43) → (44) is glossed over

Eq. (43) — as written
\[\mathbb{E}[F^{K+1}-F^K] \leq \mathbb{E}\!\left[-\eta\|\nabla F^K\|^2 - \eta e^T \nabla F^K + \frac{L\eta^2(\|\nabla F^K\|^2 + 2\|\nabla F^K e + e^2 + \xi^2)}{2}\right]\]

The phrase "using a similar mathematical logic to Sections 2.1–2.3" skips the actual algebra. Additionally, the term \(\|\nabla F^K e + e^2 + \xi^2\|\) has a dimensional problem: \(e\) is a vector, so \(e^2\) is undefined without a norm. The expression needs to be written as \(\|\nabla F^K + e\|^2 = \|\nabla F^K\|^2 + 2\nabla F^K \cdot e + \|e\|^2\) and expanded explicitly.

4. Contradictory \(\eta\) constraints

The Assumptions section sets \(\eta = \min(1/L_1, 1/L_2, 1/L_3)\). Section 2.4 later requires \(\eta \leq 1/(4L_\text{overall})\) to get a negative descent coefficient. These are compatible numerically (the second is tighter), but the proof never reconciles them. The correct statement is: the global analysis requires a smaller learning rate than the per-component analyses — this should be stated explicitly in the assumptions, not discovered mid-proof.

5. The final bound has an extra \(L_\text{overall}\eta\) factor

Eq. (47) — the variance term
\[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{K=0}^{T-1}\|\nabla F^K\|^2 \leq \frac{4(F^0 - F^*)}{\eta T} + 3e^2 + 2L_\text{overall}\,\eta\,\sigma^2_\text{overall}\]

In Sections 2.1–2.3, after the simplification \(\eta \leq 1/L\), the variance terms reduce to just \(\sigma^2\) (no \(L\) or \(\eta\) factor). The global bound retains a \(2L_\text{overall}\eta\) factor in the variance term. This inconsistency suggests an algebra error in the simplification step of Section 2.4.

What Is Entirely Missing: The Federated Learning Model

The essays and abstract describe a federated convergence theorem — multiple hospitals, data that never leaves clients, and a provably bounded cost to accuracy. But the proof is a single-machine SGD analysis applied to a three-component network. The following are absent:

  • Multiple clients: No \(n\) clients, no local datasets \(\mathcal{D}_i\), no local vs. global objective.
  • Communication rounds vs. local steps: No distinction between local gradient steps and global aggregation rounds.
  • Client drift: When clients take \(H\) local steps before aggregation, their models drift from the global model. Bounding this drift is a standard and required lemma in federated convergence proofs (cf. Li et al. 2020, FedProx).
  • Data heterogeneity \(\sigma_h^2\): The floor of the convergence bound in the essays is \(O(\beta^2 + \sigma_h^2)\). There is no \(\sigma_h\) in this proof at all.
Key mismatch: The research summary essay says "the algorithm converges at rate O(1/√T) to a neighborhood with irreducible floor O(β² + σ_h²)." The current proof gives a floor of \(3e^2 + 2L\eta\sigma^2\) with no \(\sigma_h^2\) term and no \(1/\sqrt{T}\) — it gives \(O(1/T)\). The essays and the proof are describing different theorems.

Recommended Proof Structure

Algorithm 1 (pseudocode box) BP-Free Federated Learning with Auxiliary Networks Input: n clients, T rounds, H local steps, η Assumption 1 L-smoothness (client, auxiliary, server components) Assumption 2 Bounded stochastic gradient variance (σ_b², σ_a², σ_t²) Assumption 3 Bounded auxiliary approximation error β Assumption 4 Bounded data heterogeneity σ_h² Lemma 1 (One-step descent) For one round of the BP-free auxiliary update, show: E[F^{K+1}] ≤ E[F^K] - (η/2)‖∇F^K‖² + C·β² + ... Lemma 2 (Auxiliary bias) Justify that ∇F_a is a valid surrogate for the global gradient up to error β. This is the key lemma — eq. (40) needs to live here with a full proof. Lemma 3 (Client drift) After H local steps, the average client model deviates from the global model by at most O(η²H²σ_h²). Lemma 4 (Aggregation error) FedAvg aggregation introduces heterogeneity error bounded by σ_h². Theorem 1 (1/T) Σ‖∇F^K‖² ≤ O(1/√T) + O(β²) + O(σ_h²) Proof: Apply Lemmas 1–4, telescope over T rounds, rearrange. □

The three per-component convergences (current Sections 2.1–2.3) can feed into Lemma 1 as sub-results, making them useful rather than orphaned.
