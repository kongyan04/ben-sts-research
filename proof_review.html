Section 1 — Assumptions

L-smoothness (Eqs. 1–3) — correct

Three separate smoothness constants \(L_1, L_2, L_3\) for \(F_b, F_a, F_t\) is a valid and natural choice. The paper trains three separate modules (Mb, Ca, Mt) that can have different curvature properties, so separate constants are more accurate than a single L.

Bounded variance (Eqs. 4–6) — correct

The paper uses mini-batch SGD with batch size B = 128 and momentum 0.9. The bounded stochastic gradient variance assumption is standard and compatible with this setup.

Auxiliary bias assumption (Eq. 7) — wrong quantity

Eq. 7 — as written in proof
\[\|\mathbb{E}[\nabla F_a(\theta_a;\xi_a)] - \nabla_{\theta_b}F(\theta_b,\theta_a,\theta_t)\| \leq \varepsilon\]

This is the most consequential error in the assumptions. Here is what the paper's Algorithm 1 actually does (lines 10–12):

Paper Algorithm 1, lines 10–12
Compute gradients: \(\nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux},\ \nabla_{\theta_a}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\)
Update: \(\theta_b \leftarrow \theta_b - \eta_b \nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\)
Update: \(\theta_a \leftarrow \theta_a - \eta_b \nabla_{\theta_a}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\)

The client computes TWO gradients from the same loss \(\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\):

  • \(\nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\) — gradient flowing backward through Ca and into Mb (updates the client bottom model)
  • \(\nabla_{\theta_a}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\) — gradient of just the auxiliary head's own parameters (updates Ca)

The proof's Eq. 7 compares \(\nabla F_a\) — which is \(\nabla_{\theta_a}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\), the gradient w.r.t. Ca's parameters — against \(\nabla_{\theta_b}F\), the global gradient w.r.t. the client bottom. These are gradients with respect to different parameter sets. The assumption is comparing two different objects and calling their difference small, which does not make dimensional or mathematical sense as written.

What Eq. 7 should say: The correct auxiliary bias assumption for this paper is:

\[\|\mathbb{E}[\nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}(\theta_b,\theta_a;\xi)] - \nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}(\theta_b,\theta_t;\xi)\| \leq \beta\]
This bounds how well the auxiliary pipeline gradient (what the client actually uses to update θb) approximates the true global gradient w.r.t. θb. This is the right notion of "auxiliary approximation error" for this algorithm.

Single learning rate η — simplification

The proof sets \(\eta = \min(1/L_1, 1/L_2, 1/L_3)\) globally. The paper uses \(\eta_b\) for the client and \(\eta_t\) for the server, which are independent — the client and server update simultaneously without coordinating step sizes. The single-η assumption is a simplification but not fatal; it just produces a looser bound than a two-rate analysis would.

Section 2.1 — Fb Convergence

This section is mathematically correct. The steps are:

  1. Apply L-smoothness descent lemma to \(F_b\)
  2. Substitute \(\theta_b^{K+1} = \theta_b^K - \eta\nabla F_b(\theta_b^K;\xi_b^K)\)
  3. Take expectation; use bias-variance decomposition
  4. Telescope over T rounds; rearrange

All four steps are valid. The final bound (Eq. 19) is correct:

Eq. 19 — correct
\[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{K=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla F_b(\theta_b^K)\|^2 \leq \sigma_b^2 + \frac{2L_1(F_b(\theta_b^0)-F_b^*)}{T}\]

Cross-reference with paper: The paper's client update is \(\theta_b \leftarrow \theta_b - \eta_b\nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\). This matches the update rule in Eq. 9 of the proof — as long as we interpret \(F_b(\theta_b)\) as \(\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}(C_a(M_b(x)), y)\) as a function of θb alone (treating θa as fixed). The proof doesn't state this interpretation explicitly, but it is the correct reading.

Section 2.2 — Fa Convergence

The algebra is correct, but there is a structural problem specific to this paper.

The θb / θa coupling problem

Paper (Algorithm 1, lines 9–12)
One backward pass through \(\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\) produces both gradients simultaneously:

\(\nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\) — flows backward through Ca into Mb
\(\nabla_{\theta_a}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\) — stays in Ca

θb and θa are co-optimized. They share the same loss and the same backward pass.
Proof (Sections 2.1 and 2.2)
Two independent SGD analyses:

\(\theta_b^{K+1} = \theta_b^K - \eta\nabla F_b(\theta_b^K;\xi_b^K)\)
\(\theta_a^{K+1} = \theta_a^K - \eta\nabla F_a(\theta_a^K;\xi_a^K)\)

Treated as if Fb and Fa are separate objectives with separate update rules.

In the paper, \(\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\) is a single scalar loss. When you backprop through it, you get one gradient for θb (flowing through the chain Ca ∘ Mb) and a separate gradient for θa (flowing only through Ca). These are not independent updates — they come from the same loss evaluation on the same mini-batch. The proof's structure is wrong here: it implies two separate SGD trajectories on two separate objectives, but the paper runs one joint backward pass on one objective through a two-part chain.

The correct treatment is to analyze the joint update of (θb, θa) as a single SGD step on the loss \(\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}(\theta_b, \theta_a)\), not two independent steps. The individual convergence of Fa (Eq. 29) is therefore an orphaned result — it proves Ca converges in isolation, which is not what the paper does.

The note at the end of Section 2.2 — "it stands as its own convergence, but it affects Fb when it comes to the global model convergence; the local backpropagation from Fb to Fa to update Fb in the end" — acknowledges the coupling but doesn't resolve it mathematically.

Section 2.3 — Ft Convergence

This is the strongest section of the proof. The server update in the paper is (Algorithm 1, lines 19–20):

Paper Algorithm 1 — server update
\[\theta_t \leftarrow \theta_t - \eta_t \nabla_{\theta_t}\mathcal{L}\]

This is exactly the SGD update that the Ft convergence proof (Eq. 31) analyzes. The server receives activations \(z_i\) from the client and treats them as fixed inputs for its own backward pass — matching the proof's setup. The result (Eq. 39) is correct:

Eq. 39 — correct
\[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{K=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla F_t(\theta_t^K)\|^2 \leq \sigma_t^2 + \frac{2L_3(F_t(\theta_t^0)-F_t^*)}{T}\]

One caveat: the server is optimizing on activations \(z = M_b(x)\) that change every round as θb updates. The proof treats z as fixed (which is correct within a single round) but does not account for the drift across rounds. This is the "moving target" problem discussed further in Section 2.4.

Section 2.4 — Global Convergence

Problem 1: Wrong update direction in Eq. 40

Eq. 40 — as written
\[F^{K+1} \leq F^K - \eta(\nabla F_a(\theta_a))^T\nabla F^K + \frac{L_\text{overall}}{2}\|-\eta\nabla F_a\|^2\]

This uses \(\nabla F_a(\theta_a) = \nabla_{\theta_a}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\) as the update direction for the global model F. But the paper's client uses \(\nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\) to update θb — the gradient flowing backward through Ca into Mb, with respect to Mb's parameters, not Ca's own parameters. These are different quantities:

  • \(\nabla_{\theta_a}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\) has dimension \(|\theta_a|\) — the auxiliary head's parameter space
  • \(\nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\) has dimension \(|\theta_b|\) — the client bottom model's parameter space

They live in different vector spaces. Plugging one in as a proxy for the other — and then computing an inner product with \(\nabla F^K\) which lives in the joint space — is dimensionally inconsistent.

Problem 2: Eq. 43 has a dimensional error

Eq. 43 — dimensional problem
\[\cdots + \frac{L_\text{overall}\eta^2\bigl(\|\nabla F^K\|^2 + 2\|\nabla F^K e + e^2 + \xi^2\bigr)}{2}\]

Here \(e\) is a vector (it has the same dimension as a gradient), so \(e^2\) is undefined — a vector cannot be squared without specifying an inner product or norm. The expression \(\nabla F^K e\) is also ambiguous: it could mean an inner product (scalar) or an outer product (matrix). This expression should be written as:

Corrected form
\[\|\nabla F^K + e\|^2 = \|\nabla F^K\|^2 + 2\langle\nabla F^K, e\rangle + \|e\|^2\] with the cross term bounded by Cauchy-Schwarz, then AM-GM.

The step "using a similar mathematical logic to Sections 2.1–2.3" skips expanding this explicitly, which is where the dimensional confusion enters.

Problem 3: Contradictory η constraints

The proof sets \(\eta \leq 1/L_i\) in Sections 2.1–2.3. Section 2.4 requires \(\eta \leq 1/(4L_\text{overall})\) to ensure a negative descent coefficient. Since \(L_\text{overall} = \max(L_1,L_2,L_3)\), we have \(1/(4L_\text{overall}) \leq 1/(4L_i) < 1/L_i\), so Section 2.4's constraint is strictly tighter. The proof never reconciles these: the assumptions establish one η range, and the global analysis silently switches to a tighter range mid-proof. The correct fix is to set \(\eta \leq 1/(4L_\text{overall})\) in the assumptions from the start.

Problem 4: The σ² term is inconsistent

In Sections 2.1–2.3, after setting \(\eta \leq 1/L_i\), the variance term simplifies to just \(\sigma_i^2\) with no L or η factors. The global bound (Eq. 47) ends with \(+\, 2L_\text{overall}\eta\sigma^2_\text{overall}\) — retaining both L and η. This inconsistency suggests the simplification step in Section 2.4 was not applied correctly.

Problem 5: The moving target — Mt trains on shifting activations

The server's loss at round K is \(\mathcal{L}(M_t(\mathbf{z}^K), y)\) where \(\mathbf{z}^K = M_b^K(x)\). As θb updates, \(\mathbf{z}\) changes each round. The server is therefore optimizing a sequence of related but different objectives, not a fixed one. The Ft convergence proof assumes a stationary loss landscape. Across rounds, the "target" shifts by:

Drift term (not in proof)
\[\|\mathbf{z}^{K+1} - \mathbf{z}^K\| = \|M_b^{K+1}(x) - M_b^K(x)\| \leq L_b\|\theta_b^{K+1} - \theta_b^K\| = L_b\eta_b\|\nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\|\]

This drift accumulates over T rounds and would appear as an additional error term in the final bound. It is entirely missing from the proof.

Problem 6: The component convergences are orphaned

Sections 2.1–2.3 prove that Fb, Fa, and Ft each converge independently. Section 2.4 then restarts with a completely new descent inequality (Eq. 40) and never uses Eqs. 19, 29, or 39. The three component analyses contribute nothing to the global result. A proper proof would use the component results as lemmas feeding into the global theorem.

Most important step before rewriting: The key open question is whether Assumption 3 can be derived from the paper's setup rather than just assumed. The paper's Section III.C says the auxiliary loss is "aligned with the final task since Ca tries to predict the same labels y." That informal claim needs to be formalized — specifically, under what conditions on Ca's architecture and training does \(\nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}\) approximate \(\nabla_{\theta_b}\mathcal{L}\) with bounded error? This is the central lemma the paper is missing and the proof should provide. Dr. Huang is the right person to consult on whether this is derivable or needs to remain an assumption.
