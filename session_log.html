The Regeneron Science Talent Search (STS) is the most prestigious high school science research competition in the US. Here's what it typically takes:

What Kind of Research

Winning projects tend to be:

  • Original research — not a science fair kit or replication. You must design the study yourself (or meaningfully contribute to one)
  • Lab-based — most finalists work in a university or industry research lab under a mentor (professor, MD, PhD scientist)
  • Published or publishable quality — the paper you submit should read like a journal article

Top Subject Areas (recent finalists)

  • Computational biology / bioinformatics — very competitive right now
  • Machine learning applied to science (drug discovery, genomics, climate)
  • Mathematics (pure or applied)
  • Neuroscience
  • Cancer biology / immunology
  • Physics / materials science
  • Environmental science / epidemiology

The Process

  1. Find a mentor (usually a university lab) — this is the hardest step, start cold-emailing professors early (9th/10th grade)
  2. Spend 1–3 years on a project, summers especially
  3. Write a formal research paper (the application is essentially the paper + essays)
  4. Submit senior year (deadline is usually mid-October)

What It's NOT

It's not a poster project — depth matters far more than breadth. A great project in a less trendy field (math, chemistry) can beat a mediocre ML project.

Project Abstract (draft)

Convergence Analysis of Backpropagation-Free Federated Learning via Auxiliary Networks

[Your Name] · Advisor: Dr. Chao Huang · Montclair State University

Background. Federated learning enables multiple clients — such as hospitals or mobile devices — to collaboratively train neural networks without sharing raw data. Standard federated algorithms (e.g., FedAvg) rely on backpropagation (BP), which requires transmitting full gradient information, raising both communication and privacy concerns. Backpropagation-free learning via auxiliary networks replaces global gradient flow with local learning signals, but its convergence behavior in a federated setting remains theoretically uncharacterized.

Objective. This work establishes the first convergence guarantees for BP-free auxiliary network training under federated, non-IID data conditions.

Methods. We model each client as minimizing a local objective \(F_i\) over heterogeneous data distributions, with global objective \(F = \frac{1}{N}\sum F_i\). Each client runs \(K\) local steps using auxiliary network gradient approximations rather than true backpropagated gradients. We prove convergence under four standard assumptions: \(L\)-smoothness, bounded stochastic variance, bounded auxiliary approximation error \(\|\delta_i\| \leq \beta\), and bounded client heterogeneity \(\sigma_h\).

Main Result. After \(T\) communication rounds: \[\frac{1}{T}\sum_t \mathbb{E}\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 \;\leq\; O\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\right) + O(\beta^2) + O(\sigma_h^2)\]

Significance. The result reveals a precise tradeoff: better auxiliary networks (smaller \(\beta\)) directly tighten the convergence bound, giving theoretical guidance for auxiliary network design. When \(\beta \to 0\), the bound recovers the standard FedAvg convergence rate.

Proof Structure Roadmap

  • Lemma 1 — One-Step Descent (auxiliary gradient)
  • Lemma 2 — Client Drift (\(K\) local steps)
  • Lemma 3 — Aggregation Error
  • Lemma 4 — Telescoping → Main Theorem

Lemma 1 — Full Proof

Lemma 1 (One-Step Descent with Auxiliary Gradient) Under Assumptions 1–2, with \(\eta \leq \frac{1}{4L}\): \[F(w_{t+1}) \leq F(w_t) - \frac{\eta}{4}\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 + C\beta^2\] where \(C = \eta\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2} + L\eta\right)\).
Proof

Step 1 — Descent Lemma.

\[F(w_{t+1}) \leq F(w_t) - \eta\langle\nabla F(w_t),\,g_t\rangle + \frac{L\eta^2}{2}\|g_t\|^2\]

Step 2 — Lower-bound inner product.

Write \(g_t = \nabla F(w_t) + \delta_t\), \(\|\delta_t\|\leq\beta\). By Young's inequality:

\[\langle\nabla F(w_t),\,g_t\rangle \geq \frac{1}{2}\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 - \frac{\beta^2}{2}\]

Step 3 — Upper-bound the norm.

\[\|g_t\|^2 \leq 2\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 + 2\beta^2\]

Step 4 — Combine, apply \(\eta \leq \frac{1}{4L}\).

\[F(w_{t+1}) \leq F(w_t) - \frac{\eta}{4}\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 + C\beta^2 \qquad \square\]

Interpretation. When \(\beta=0\) this recovers the classical descent lemma. Far from a stationary point, the true gradient signal dominates the auxiliary error.

Section 1 — Introduction

Deep neural networks have achieved remarkable success across scientific domains, from protein structure prediction to climate modeling and drug discovery. However, the standard training algorithm — backpropagation (BP) — carries fundamental limitations that hinder its deployment in distributed and privacy-sensitive settings. BP requires a global, sequential backward pass through the entire network, making it biologically implausible, memory-intensive, and incompatible with settings where different layers reside on different machines or institutions.

Federated learning [McMahan et al., 2017] addresses the data-sharing problem by allowing clients — such as hospitals, research labs, or edge devices — to train locally and share only model updates with a central aggregator. Yet standard federated algorithms such as FedAvg still rely on backpropagation within each client. A natural question arises: can we train federated neural networks without backpropagation at all?

Backpropagation-free learning via auxiliary networks [Huang et al., 2025] offers a promising alternative. In this framework, each layer receives a local learning signal from a small auxiliary network rather than a global backpropagated gradient. However, the theoretical convergence behavior of auxiliary network training in federated, non-IID settings has not been established.

This work closes that gap. Our contributions are:

  1. A formal convergence guarantee (Theorem 1): federated auxiliary network training converges at rate \(O(1/\sqrt{T})\) to a neighborhood controlled by \(\beta\) and \(\sigma_h\).
  2. A tight auxiliary quality lower bound characterizing exactly how precise auxiliary networks must be.
  3. A unified framework that recovers classical FedAvg convergence as \(\beta \to 0\).

Section 2 — Related Work

2.1 Backpropagation-Free and Local Learning Rules. Feedback alignment [Lillicrap et al., 2016; Nøkland, 2016] replaces the transposed weight matrix with fixed random matrices. Target propagation [Lee et al., 2015] propagates target activations rather than error gradients. Greedy layer-wise methods [Belilovsky et al., 2019; Nøkland & Eidnes, 2019] train layers sequentially with local loss functions. Hinton [2022] introduced the Forward-Forward algorithm. Huang et al. [2025] formalize the auxiliary network framework in the single-machine, IID setting. The present paper extends that analysis to the federated, non-IID setting.

2.2 Federated Learning and Non-IID Convergence. McMahan et al. [2017] introduced FedAvg. Li et al. [2020] provided the first rigorous convergence analysis under non-IID conditions, establishing \(O(1/\sqrt{T})\) rate with a bias term proportional to data heterogeneity. Karimireddy et al. [2020] introduced SCAFFOLD using control variates to correct client drift. All these analyses assume standard backpropagation. Our work is the first to analyze federated convergence when clients use auxiliary network gradient approximations.

2.3 Split and Distributed Neural Networks. Vepakomma et al. [2018] propose split learning, where intermediate representations are transmitted. Our setting is complementary: rather than splitting the architecture, we eliminate the backward pass entirely, so no gradients need to cross the client-server boundary — strengthening privacy beyond what split learning alone achieves.

2.4 Convergence Under Approximate Gradients. Ajalloeian & Stich [2020] study SGD with biased gradient oracles, showing convergence to a neighborhood proportional to the bias. The key distinction: auxiliary error \(\beta\) is structured and controllable — depending on auxiliary network capacity and training — enabling the design insight in Section 5.

Lemma 2 — Client Drift Bound

Lemma 2 (Client Drift Bound) Under Assumptions 1–3, after \(K\) local steps starting from shared \(w_t\): \[\left\|w^{(i)}_{t,K} - w_t\right\|^2 \leq K^2\eta^2(G+\beta)^2\]
Proof

Step 1 — Telescope.

\[w^{(i)}_{t,K} - w_t = -\eta\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}g^{(i)}_{t,k}\]

Step 2 — Bound each auxiliary gradient.

\[\|g^{(i)}_{t,k}\| \leq \|\nabla F_i(w^{(i)}_{t,k})\| + \|\delta^{(i)}_{t,k}\| \leq G + \beta\]

Step 3 — Triangle inequality + square.

\[\|w^{(i)}_{t,K} - w_t\| \leq K\eta(G+\beta) \;\Rightarrow\; \|w^{(i)}_{t,K}-w_t\|^2 \leq K^2\eta^2(G+\beta)^2 \qquad \square\]

Key insight. \(\beta\) and \(G\) contribute additively to drift — a poor auxiliary network acts as if each client has a systematically larger effective gradient. The \(K^2\) growth motivates limiting local steps when heterogeneity is high.

Section 5 — Design Implications

To achieve \(\varepsilon\)-accuracy (\(\|\nabla F(w)\|^2\leq\varepsilon\)), Theorem 1 requires two simultaneous conditions:

ConditionRequirement
Enough rounds\(T \geq \frac{64L^2(F(w_0)-F^*)^2}{\varepsilon^2}\)
Auxiliary quality\(\beta \leq \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{2C_1}}\)
Corollary 5.1 To achieve \(\varepsilon\)-accuracy, auxiliary networks must satisfy \(\beta = O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\). Halving the target accuracy requires reducing \(\beta\) by only \(1/\sqrt{2}\) — a favorable square-root relationship.
Corollary 5.2 (Binding Constraint Threshold) When \(\beta^2 < \sigma_h^2\), improving auxiliary networks yields no further benefit. The practitioner should invest in data alignment rather than model improvement.

Optimal local steps: \(K^* = \left(\frac{\sigma_h^2+\beta^2}{L^2\eta^2(G+\beta)^2}\right)^{1/2}\). Near-IID clients and strong auxiliary networks allow many local steps safely; high heterogeneity favors frequent aggregation.

Section 3 — Problem Setup & Assumptions

Federated objective: \(F(w) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N F_i(w)\), where \(F_i(w) = \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim D_i}[\ell(f_w(x),y)]\). Clients never share raw data; only model weights are transmitted.

BP-Free local update rule — at each local step \(k\), for each layer \(\ell\):

\[\text{(i)}\; h^{(\ell)}_{t,k} = \sigma(W_\ell\cdot\mathrm{sg}(h^{(\ell-1)}_{t,k})), \quad \text{(ii)}\; \mathcal{L}^{(\ell)}_{t,k} = \ell(A_\ell(h^{(\ell)}_{t,k}),y), \quad \text{(iii)}\; w^{(i)}_{\ell,k+1} = w^{(i)}_{\ell,k} - \eta\nabla_{w_\ell}\mathcal{L}^{(\ell)}_{t,k}\]

where \(\mathrm{sg}(\cdot)\) is stop-gradient (detach). No gradient flows between layers. After \(K\) steps: \(w_{t+1}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i w^{(i)}_{t,K}\).

Assumptions: (1) \(L\)-Smoothness, (2) Bounded Auxiliary Error \(\|\delta\|\leq\beta\), (3) Bounded Local Gradients \(\|\nabla F_i\|\leq G\), (4) Bounded Heterogeneity \(\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\|\nabla F_i(w)-\nabla F(w)\|^2\leq\sigma_h^2\). Assumption 2 is the only novel one introduced by this paper.

Section 7 — Conclusion

We established the first convergence guarantees for backpropagation-free neural network training in a federated, non-IID setting. Under four standard assumptions, federated auxiliary network training converges at rate \(O(1/\sqrt{T})\) to a neighborhood of a stationary point, with irreducible floor \(O(\beta^2+\sigma_h^2)\). The proof proceeds through four lemmas establishing Theorem 1, which recovers classical FedAvg convergence exactly when \(\beta\to 0\). From the bound we derived actionable design rules: auxiliary networks must satisfy \(\beta=O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\) for \(\varepsilon\)-accuracy, and improving auxiliary quality yields no benefit once \(\beta^2\) falls below the heterogeneity floor \(\sigma_h^2\). Numerical experiments validate all three components of the bound independently. Beyond convergence, the BP-free federated framework offers a structural privacy advantage: no gradient is transmitted across the client-server boundary. Future work includes partial client participation, optimal auxiliary network architecture, and empirical validation on MIMIC-IV.

"""
Experiment 1: Federated BP-Free Learning — Convergence Rate Validation
Validates O(1/√T) convergence from Theorem 1.
Install: pip install torch torchvision matplotlib numpy
Run:     python experiment1_convergence.py
"""

import copy, numpy as np, torch, torch.nn as nn, torch.nn.functional as F
from torch.utils.data import DataLoader, Subset
import torchvision, torchvision.transforms as transforms
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

torch.manual_seed(42); np.random.seed(42)

N_CLIENTS = 10; ROUNDS = 200; K = 5; LR = 0.01
BATCH_SIZE = 32; AUX_WIDTH = 64; ALPHA = 100.0; N_CLASSES = 10
DEVICE = torch.device("cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu")
MEASURE_EVERY = 5

tfm = transforms.Compose([transforms.ToTensor(),
                           transforms.Normalize((0.1307,),(0.3081,))])
train_data = torchvision.datasets.MNIST("./data",train=True, download=True,transform=tfm)
test_data  = torchvision.datasets.MNIST("./data",train=False,download=True,transform=tfm)

def dirichlet_partition(dataset, n_clients, alpha):
    labels = np.array([dataset[i][1] for i in range(len(dataset))])
    client_idx = [[] for _ in range(n_clients)]
    for c in range(N_CLASSES):
        cls_idx = np.where(labels == c)[0]; np.random.shuffle(cls_idx)
        props = np.random.dirichlet([alpha]*n_clients)
        splits = (props*len(cls_idx)).astype(int)
        splits[-1] = len(cls_idx)-splits[:-1].sum()
        ptr = 0
        for i,sz in enumerate(splits):
            client_idx[i].extend(cls_idx[ptr:ptr+sz].tolist()); ptr += sz
    return client_idx

client_idx = dirichlet_partition(train_data, N_CLIENTS, ALPHA)
loaders    = [DataLoader(Subset(train_data,idx),BATCH_SIZE,shuffle=True,drop_last=True)
              for idx in client_idx]

class MainNet(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__()
        self.fc1=nn.Linear(784,256); self.fc2=nn.Linear(256,128); self.fc3=nn.Linear(128,N_CLASSES)
    def forward(self,x):
        x=x.view(-1,784); return self.fc3(F.relu(self.fc2(F.relu(self.fc1(x)))))
    def forward_layerwise(self,x):
        x=x.view(-1,784)
        h1=F.relu(self.fc1(x)); h2=F.relu(self.fc2(h1.detach())); return h1,h2

class AuxNet(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self,d_in):
        super().__init__()
        self.net=nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(d_in,AUX_WIDTH),nn.ReLU(),nn.Linear(AUX_WIDTH,N_CLASSES))
    def forward(self,h): return self.net(h)

class BPFreeClient:
    def __init__(self,global_weights):
        self.main=MainNet().to(DEVICE); self.main.load_state_dict(global_weights)
        self.aux1=AuxNet(256).to(DEVICE); self.aux2=AuxNet(128).to(DEVICE)
        self.opt1=torch.optim.SGD(list(self.main.fc1.parameters())+list(self.aux1.parameters()),lr=LR)
        self.opt2=torch.optim.SGD(list(self.main.fc2.parameters())+list(self.aux2.parameters()),lr=LR)
        self.opt3=torch.optim.SGD(self.main.fc3.parameters(),lr=LR)

    def train_round(self,loader):
        self.main.train()
        it = iter(loader)
        for _ in range(K):
            try: x,y = next(it)
            except StopIteration: it=iter(loader); x,y=next(it)
            x,y = x.to(DEVICE), y.to(DEVICE)
            h1,h2 = self.main.forward_layerwise(x)
            # Layer 1
            self.opt1.zero_grad()
            F.cross_entropy(self.aux1(h1),y).backward(); self.opt1.step()
            # Layer 2
            self.opt2.zero_grad()
            F.cross_entropy(self.aux2(h2),y).backward(); self.opt2.step()
            # Layer 3
            self.opt3.zero_grad()
            F.cross_entropy(self.main.fc3(h2.detach()),y).backward(); self.opt3.step()

    def get_weights(self): return copy.deepcopy(self.main.state_dict())

def fedavg(weight_list):
    avg = copy.deepcopy(weight_list[0])
    for k in avg: avg[k] = torch.stack([w[k].float() for w in weight_list]).mean(0)
    return avg

def grad_norm(model,loaders):
    model.train()
    total = sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters())
    grads = torch.zeros(total).to(DEVICE)
    for loader in loaders:
        x,y = next(iter(loader)); x,y = x.to(DEVICE),y.to(DEVICE)
        model.zero_grad()
        F.cross_entropy(model(x),y).backward()
        grads += torch.cat([p.grad.view(-1) for p in model.parameters() if p.grad is not None])
    return (grads/len(loaders)).norm().item()**2

global_model = MainNet().to(DEVICE)
global_weights = global_model.state_dict()
grad_norms, rounds_measured = [], []

for rnd in range(ROUNDS):
    client_weights = []
    for loader in loaders:
        c = BPFreeClient(global_weights)
        c.train_round(loader)
        client_weights.append(c.get_weights())
    global_weights = fedavg(client_weights)
    global_model.load_state_dict(global_weights)
    if rnd % MEASURE_EVERY == 0:
        gn = grad_norm(global_model, loaders)
        grad_norms.append(gn); rounds_measured.append(rnd+1)
        print(f"Round {rnd+1:3d}  grad_norm²={gn:.4f}")

# Log-log plot
log_r = np.log(rounds_measured); log_g = np.log(np.maximum(grad_norms,1e-8))
slope, intercept = np.polyfit(log_r, log_g, 1)
print(f"\nLog-log slope: {slope:.3f}  (theory: -0.50)")
plt.figure(figsize=(6,4))
plt.loglog(rounds_measured, grad_norms, 'b-o', ms=4, label='BP-Free Fed')
t_ref = np.array(rounds_measured)
plt.loglog(t_ref, np.exp(intercept)*t_ref**(-0.5), 'r--', alpha=0.7, label='O(1/√T) ref')
plt.xlabel('Round (log scale)'); plt.ylabel('‖∇F‖² (log scale)')
plt.title(f'Convergence Rate Validation (slope={slope:.2f})'); plt.legend(); plt.grid(True,alpha=0.3)
plt.tight_layout(); plt.savefig('experiment1_convergence.png',dpi=150); plt.show()
print("Saved: experiment1_convergence.png")
