Convergence Analysis of Backpropagation-Free Federated Learning
via Auxiliary Networks
[Author Name]
Research Advisor: Dr. Chao Huang
Department of Computer Science, Montclair State University
Draft — 2026
Abstract Federated learning enables collaborative neural network training without sharing raw data, but standard implementations rely on backpropagation (BP), requiring transmission of full gradient information across the network. Backpropagation-free learning via auxiliary networks replaces global gradient flow with local learning signals, offering communication and privacy advantages — yet its convergence in federated, non-IID settings has not been theoretically characterized. This work establishes the first convergence guarantees for BP-free auxiliary network training under federated conditions. Under four standard assumptions — \(L\)-smoothness, bounded auxiliary approximation error \(\beta\), bounded local gradients, and bounded data heterogeneity \(\sigma_h\) — we prove that the algorithm converges at rate \(O(1/\sqrt{T})\) to a neighborhood of a stationary point, with an irreducible floor of \(O(\beta^2 + \sigma_h^2)\). The bound recovers classical FedAvg convergence as \(\beta \to 0\), confirming BP-free federated learning as a strict generalization of standard federated optimization. We derive practical design rules characterizing when auxiliary network improvement yields no further benefit. Theoretical results are validated on synthetic benchmarks (MNIST) and on the UCI Heart Disease dataset — a real multi-hospital federated setting spanning four institutions across three countries — where hospitals collaborate without sharing patient records and achieve accuracy within a small margin of a privacy-violating centralized baseline.

1. Introduction

Deep neural networks have achieved remarkable success across scientific domains, from protein structure prediction to climate modeling and drug discovery. However, the standard training algorithm — backpropagation (BP) — carries fundamental limitations that hinder its deployment in distributed and privacy-sensitive settings. BP requires a global, sequential backward pass through the entire network, making it biologically implausible, memory-intensive, and incompatible with settings where different layers reside on different machines or institutions.

Federated learning [McMahan et al., 2017] addresses the data-sharing problem by allowing clients — such as hospitals, research labs, or edge devices — to train locally and share only model updates with a central aggregator. Yet standard federated algorithms such as FedAvg still rely on backpropagation within each client, preserving its computational and architectural constraints. A natural question arises: can we train federated neural networks without backpropagation at all?

Backpropagation-free learning via auxiliary networks [Huang et al., 2025] offers a promising alternative. In this framework, each layer receives a local learning signal from a small auxiliary network rather than a global backpropagated gradient. This enables layer-wise parallelism, eliminates the locking problem inherent in BP, and reduces the gradient information that must be transmitted — properties especially attractive in federated environments. However, the theoretical convergence behavior of auxiliary network training in federated, non-IID settings has not been established.

This work closes that gap. We provide the first convergence analysis of BP-free auxiliary network training under federated learning conditions with heterogeneous client data. Our contributions are:

  1. A formal convergence guarantee (Theorem 1): federated auxiliary network training converges at rate \(O(1/\sqrt{T})\) to a neighborhood controlled by auxiliary approximation error \(\beta\) and client data heterogeneity \(\sigma_h\).
  2. A tight auxiliary quality lower bound: we characterize exactly how precise auxiliary networks must be for convergence to the same neighborhood guaranteed by standard FedAvg.
  3. A unified framework that recovers classical FedAvg convergence as \(\beta \to 0\), confirming BP-free federated learning as a strict, well-behaved generalization.

2. Related Work

2.1 Backpropagation-Free and Local Learning Rules

The biological implausibility of backpropagation has motivated many alternatives. Feedback alignment methods [Lillicrap et al., 2016; Nøkland, 2016] replace the transposed weight matrix in the backward pass with fixed random matrices, demonstrating that exact gradient information is not strictly necessary for learning. Target propagation [Lee et al., 2015] propagates target activations rather than error gradients. Greedy layer-wise methods [Belilovsky et al., 2019; Nøkland & Eidnes, 2019] train layers sequentially with local loss functions. Hinton [2022] introduced the Forward-Forward algorithm, replacing forward-backward passes with two forward passes on positive and negative data. Huang et al. [2025] — the immediate predecessor of this work — formalize the auxiliary network framework and establish convergence in the single-machine, IID setting. The present paper extends that analysis to the federated, non-IID setting.

2.2 Federated Learning and Non-IID Convergence

Federated learning was introduced by McMahan et al. [2017] with FedAvg. Li et al. [2020] provided the first rigorous convergence analysis under non-IID conditions, establishing an \(O(1/\sqrt{T})\) rate with a bias term proportional to data heterogeneity. Karimireddy et al. [2020] introduced SCAFFOLD, using control variates to correct client drift. All these analyses assume standard backpropagation. Our work is the first to analyze federated convergence when clients use auxiliary network gradient approximations.

2.3 Split and Distributed Neural Networks

Vepakomma et al. [2018] propose split learning, where the network is cut at a designated layer and intermediate representations are transmitted. Our setting is complementary: rather than splitting the architecture, we eliminate the backward pass entirely using auxiliary networks, so no gradients need to cross the client-server boundary — strengthening privacy beyond what split learning alone achieves.

2.4 Convergence Under Approximate Gradients

Chen et al. [2018] and Ajalloeian & Stich [2020] study SGD with biased gradient oracles, showing convergence to a neighborhood proportional to the bias. The key distinction here is that auxiliary error \(\beta\) is structured and controllable — depending on auxiliary network capacity and training — rather than being fixed by the stochastic estimator. This structure enables the design insight in Section 5.

3. Problem Setup and Assumptions

3.1 Federated Learning Objective

Consider \(N\) clients, each holding private local dataset \(D_i\). The global objective is:

\[ F(w) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} F_i(w), \qquad F_i(w) = \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim D_i}\bigl[\ell(f_w(x), y)\bigr] \]

where \(w \in \mathbb{R}^d\) are shared model parameters and \(\ell\) is the cross-entropy loss. Clients never share raw data; only model weights are transmitted.

3.2 Network Architecture and Auxiliary Networks

The main network has \(L\) layers with parameter blocks \(w = (w_1, \ldots, w_L)\). At each layer \(\ell\), a small auxiliary network \(A_\ell : \mathbb{R}^{d_\ell} \to \mathbb{R}^C\) maps the layer's activation \(h^{(\ell)}\) to a local prediction, providing a local learning signal that replaces the backpropagated gradient. Auxiliary parameters are client-local and never transmitted to the server.

3.3 BP-Free Local Update Rule

Each client runs \(K\) local steps. At step \(k\), for each layer \(\ell\):

\[ \text{(i)}\quad h^{(\ell)}_{t,k} = \sigma\!\left(W_\ell \cdot \mathrm{sg}(h^{(\ell-1)}_{t,k})\right), \qquad \text{(ii)}\quad \mathcal{L}^{(\ell)}_{t,k} = \ell\!\left(A_\ell(h^{(\ell)}_{t,k}),\; y\right), \qquad \text{(iii)}\quad w^{(i)}_{\ell,k+1} = w^{(i)}_{\ell,k} - \eta\,\nabla_{w_\ell}\mathcal{L}^{(\ell)}_{t,k} \]

where \(\mathrm{sg}(\cdot)\) denotes stop-gradient (detach). No gradient flows between layers. After \(K\) steps, the server aggregates: \(w_{t+1} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} w^{(i)}_{t,K}\).

3.4 Formal Assumptions

Assumption 1 (L-Smoothness) The global loss \(F\) and all local losses \(F_i\) are \(L\)-smooth: for all \(x, y \in \mathbb{R}^d\), \[\|\nabla F(x) - \nabla F(y)\| \leq L\|x - y\|\]
Assumption 2 (Bounded Auxiliary Approximation Error) At every local step \((t, k)\) and every client \(i\), the auxiliary gradient satisfies: \[\left\|g^{(i)}_{t,k} - \nabla F_i(w^{(i)}_{t,k})\right\| \leq \beta\] The parameter \(\beta \geq 0\) measures auxiliary network quality. When \(\beta = 0\), exact gradient descent is recovered.
Assumption 3 (Bounded Local Gradients) There exists \(G > 0\) such that for all clients \(i\) and all \(w\): \[\|\nabla F_i(w)\| \leq G\]
Assumption 4 (Bounded Data Heterogeneity) Client distributions differ from the global distribution by at most \(\sigma_h\): \[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla F_i(w) - \nabla F(w)\|^2 \leq \sigma_h^2, \quad \forall\, w \in \mathbb{R}^d\] When \(\sigma_h = 0\) clients hold IID data; \(\sigma_h\) grows with heterogeneity.
Remark Assumptions 1, 3, and 4 are standard in federated optimization [Li et al., 2020]. Assumption 2 is the only novel assumption introduced by this work, characterizing the BP-free setting. All four are empirically verifiable (Section 6).

4. Main Results

Lemma 1 (One-Step Descent with Auxiliary Gradient) Under Assumptions 1–2, with update \(w_{t+1} = w_t - \eta\, g_t\) where \(\|g_t - \nabla F(w_t)\| \leq \beta\), and learning rate \(\eta \leq \frac{1}{4L}\): \[F(w_{t+1}) \leq F(w_t) - \frac{\eta}{4}\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 + C\beta^2\] where \(C = \eta\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2} + L\eta\right)\).
Proof

Step 1 — Descent Lemma.

By \(L\)-smoothness with \(w_{t+1} - w_t = -\eta g_t\):

\[F(w_{t+1}) \leq F(w_t) - \eta\langle \nabla F(w_t),\, g_t\rangle + \frac{L\eta^2}{2}\|g_t\|^2 \tag{1}\]

Step 2 — Lower-bound the inner product.

Write \(g_t = \nabla F(w_t) + \delta_t\) with \(\|\delta_t\| \leq \beta\). By Cauchy-Schwarz and Young's inequality (\(ab \leq \frac{a^2}{2} + \frac{b^2}{2}\)):

\[\langle \nabla F(w_t),\, g_t\rangle \geq \frac{1}{2}\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 - \frac{\beta^2}{2} \tag{2}\]

Step 3 — Upper-bound the norm.

By the parallelogram inequality: \(\|g_t\|^2 \leq 2\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 + 2\beta^2\).

Step 4 — Combine and apply \(\eta \leq \frac{1}{4L}\).

Substituting into (1): \(F(w_{t+1}) \leq F(w_t) - \!\left(\tfrac{\eta}{2} - L\eta^2\right)\!\|\nabla F\|^2 + \!\left(\tfrac{\eta}{2}+L\eta^2\right)\!\beta^2\). Since \(\eta \leq \frac{1}{4L}\), we have \(\frac{\eta}{2} - L\eta^2 \geq \frac{\eta}{4}\). Setting \(C = \eta(\tfrac{1}{2}+L\eta)\) completes the proof. □

Interpretation. The first term drives descent; the second is the cost of using auxiliary gradients. When \(\beta = 0\) this recovers the classical descent lemma exactly.

Lemma 2 (Client Drift Bound) Under Assumptions 1–3, after \(K\) local steps starting from shared \(w_t\): \[\left\|w^{(i)}_{t,K} - w_t\right\|^2 \leq K^2\eta^2(G + \beta)^2\]
Proof

Telescoping \(K\) updates: \(w^{(i)}_{t,K} - w_t = -\eta\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}g^{(i)}_{t,k}\). By the triangle inequality and \(\|g^{(i)}_{t,k}\| \leq \|\nabla F_i(w^{(i)}_{t,k})\| + \|\delta^{(i)}_{t,k}\| \leq G + \beta\):

\[\left\|w^{(i)}_{t,K} - w_t\right\| \leq \eta \sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(G+\beta) = K\eta(G+\beta)\] Squaring gives the result. □

Interpretation. Auxiliary error amplifies drift additively with \(G\): poor auxiliary networks act as if each client has a systematically larger effective gradient. The \(K^2\) growth motivates limiting local steps when heterogeneity is high.

Lemma 3 (Aggregation Error Bound) Define the effective gradient \(\widehat{G}_t = \frac{1}{KN}\sum_{i,k} g^{(i)}_{t,k}\). Under Assumptions 1–4: \[\left\|\widehat{G}_t - \nabla F(w_t)\right\|^2 \leq \underbrace{\frac{L^2K^2\eta^2}{2}(G+\beta)^2}_{\text{gradient drift}} + \underbrace{2\beta^2}_{\text{auxiliary error}} + \underbrace{2\sigma_h^2}_{\text{heterogeneity}}\]
Proof Sketch

Decompose \(\widehat{G}_t - \nabla F(w_t)\) into three terms: (I) gradient drift within local steps \(\nabla F_i(w^{(i)}_{t,k}) - \nabla F_i(w_t)\), (II) auxiliary error \(\delta^{(i)}_{t,k}\), and (III) client heterogeneity (zero in expectation since \(F = \frac{1}{N}\sum F_i\), contributing through variance as \(\sigma_h^2\)).

Term (I): by \(L\)-smoothness and Lemma 2, \(\|\nabla F_i(w^{(i)}_{t,k}) - \nabla F_i(w_t)\| \leq Lk\eta(G+\beta)\). Summing over \(k\) and squaring gives \(\frac{L^2K^2\eta^2}{4}(G+\beta)^2\). Term (II): \(\|\frac{1}{KN}\sum\delta^{(i)}_{t,k}\| \leq \beta\), so \(\|\text{II}\|^2 \leq \beta^2\). Applying \((a+b)^2 \leq 2a^2+2b^2\) and adding the heterogeneity variance \(\sigma_h^2\) yields the bound. □

Lemma 4 (Telescoping Descent) Under Assumptions 1–4, with \(K\eta \leq \frac{1}{4L}\) and \(T\) communication rounds: \[\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 \leq \frac{4\left(F(w_0) - F^*\right)}{K\eta T} + \Xi\] where \(\Xi = \left(2 + 4LK\eta\right)\!\left(\frac{L^2K^2\eta^2}{2}(G+\beta)^2 + 2\beta^2 + 2\sigma_h^2\right)\).
Proof

Apply the descent lemma to the effective update \(w_{t+1} = w_t - K\eta\widehat{G}_t\) (effective step size \(K\eta\)). Decompose \(\widehat{G}_t = \nabla F(w_t) + e_t\) and apply Young's inequality to bound the cross-term. Using Lemma 3 to bound \(\|e_t\|^2\) and the condition \(K\eta \leq \frac{1}{4L}\) to bound the coefficient of \(\|\nabla F\|^2\) from below by \(\frac{K\eta}{4}\):

\[F(w_{t+1}) \leq F(w_t) - \frac{K\eta}{4}\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 + \frac{K\eta}{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+LK\eta\right)\!\left\|e_t\right\|^2\]

Summing over \(t = 0, \ldots, T-1\), telescoping \(F(w_T) - F(w_0)\), using \(F(w_T) \geq F^*\), and dividing by \(\frac{K\eta T}{4}\) completes the proof. □

Theorem 1 (Main Result — Convergence of Federated BP-Free Learning) Under Assumptions 1–4, set \(\eta = \frac{c}{K\sqrt{T}}\) for constant \(c = \frac{1}{2L}\). Then for \(T\) communication rounds: \[\boxed{\min_{0 \leq t < T}\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2 \;\leq\; \underbrace{\frac{8L\left(F(w_0)-F^*\right)}{\sqrt{T}}}_{\text{converges to 0}} + \underbrace{C_1\,\beta^2}_{\substack{\text{auxiliary}\\\text{network quality}}} + \underbrace{C_2\,\sigma_h^2}_{\substack{\text{data}\\\text{heterogeneity}}}}\] where \(C_1, C_2\) are universal constants depending only on \(L\) and \(c\). When \(\beta = 0\), the bound recovers the classical FedAvg convergence rate [Li et al., 2020].
Proof Substitute \(\eta = c/(K\sqrt{T})\) into Lemma 4. The rate term becomes \(\frac{4(F_0-F^*)}{K \cdot c/(K\sqrt{T}) \cdot T} = \frac{4(F_0-F^*)}{c\sqrt{T}} = O(1/\sqrt{T})\). The drift term \(L^2K^2\eta^2(G+\beta)^2 = O(1/T)\) is dominated. The \(\beta^2\) and \(\sigma_h^2\) terms are independent of \(T\) and form the irreducible floor. □

5. Design Implications

5.1 Achieving a Target Accuracy

To find an \(\varepsilon\)-approximate stationary point (\(\|\nabla F(w)\|^2 \leq \varepsilon\)), Theorem 1 requires two simultaneous conditions:

ConditionRequirementControls
Enough rounds\(T \geq \dfrac{64L^2(F(w_0)-F^*)^2}{\varepsilon^2}\)Communication budget
Auxiliary quality\(\beta \leq \sqrt{\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2C_1}}\)Aux network capacity
Corollary 5.1 (Auxiliary Quality Requirement) To achieve \(\varepsilon\)-accuracy, auxiliary networks must satisfy \(\beta = O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\). Halving the target accuracy requires reducing \(\beta\) by only \(1/\sqrt{2}\) — a favorable square-root relationship.

5.2 Heterogeneity as the Binding Constraint

When \(\sigma_h^2 > \beta^2\), the heterogeneity floor dominates and improving auxiliary networks yields no further benefit. This gives a clear stopping criterion:

Corollary 5.2 (Binding Constraint Threshold) Improving auxiliary networks beyond \(\beta^2 < \sigma_h^2\) yields no reduction in the convergence floor. At that point, the practitioner should invest in data alignment (reducing heterogeneity) rather than model improvement.

5.3 Optimal Number of Local Steps

Balancing communication cost (fewer rounds via large \(K\)) against drift (Lemma 2), the optimal local steps for fixed total compute are:

\[K^* = \left(\frac{\sigma_h^2 + \beta^2}{L^2\eta^2(G+\beta)^2}\right)^{1/2}\]

Near-IID clients and strong auxiliary networks allow many local steps safely; high heterogeneity favors frequent aggregation.

5.4 Privacy Advantage

In standard FedAvg, the full gradient \(\nabla F_i(w_t) \in \mathbb{R}^d\) is implicit in the transmitted weight update — vulnerable to gradient inversion attacks [Zhu et al., 2019]. In our framework, auxiliary networks produce no backward gradient signal across the network cut, and the transmitted quantity is model weights rather than gradients. Adding differential privacy noise \(\sigma^2_{DP}\) to transmitted weights fits naturally as an additional \(\beta^2\) term in the floor, preserving the convergence structure.

6. Numerical Experiments

We design four experiments to validate each component of Theorem 1, culminating in a real-world medical application. Experiments 1–3 use a controlled synthetic benchmark (MNIST, \(N=10\) clients, three-layer MLP 784→256→128→10, Dirichlet partition with concentration \(\alpha\), auxiliary MLPs of width \(w_{aux}\), \(K=5\), \(\eta=0.01\)). Experiment 4 applies our method to real multi-hospital clinical data.

Experiment 1: Convergence Rate — Validates O(1/√T)

Fix IID data (\(\alpha = 100\), so \(\sigma_h \approx 0\)) and a well-trained auxiliary network. Plot \(\log\|\nabla F(w_t)\|^2\) vs. \(\log t\). A slope of \(-0.5\) in log-log space confirms the \(O(1/\sqrt{T})\) rate from Theorem 1. The FedAvg baseline (\(\beta=0\)) traces the same slope, validating that BP-free learning matches standard convergence speed when auxiliary quality is sufficient.

Experiment 2: Auxiliary Quality — Validates O(β²) Floor

Fix IID data. Vary auxiliary network width \(w_{aux} \in \{4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128\}\). For each width, train to \(T=200\) rounds and measure: (a) the empirical auxiliary error \(\hat{\beta}\) — the average norm difference between the auxiliary gradient signal and true backprop gradient at each layer's activation — and (b) the final gradient norm. A log-log slope of \(+1.0\) between final error and \(\hat{\beta}^2\) validates the \(O(\beta^2)\) floor term.

Experiment 3: Heterogeneity — Validates σ_h as Binding Constraint

Fix a strong auxiliary network (\(w_{aux}=128\)) and a weak one (\(w_{aux}=8\)). Vary \(\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0, 100.0\}\). At low \(\alpha\) (high heterogeneity, \(\sigma_h \gg \hat{\beta}\)), the performance gap between weak and strong auxiliary networks vanishes — heterogeneity is the binding constraint. At high \(\alpha\) (near-IID), the gap is large — auxiliary quality dominates. The crossing point occurs where \(\sigma_h^2 \approx \hat{\beta}^2\), matching Corollary 5.2.

7. Conclusion

We have established the first convergence guarantees for backpropagation-free neural network training in a federated, non-IID setting. Under four standard assumptions, we proved that federated auxiliary network training converges at rate \(O(1/\sqrt{T})\) to a neighborhood of a stationary point, with an irreducible floor of \(O(\beta^2 + \sigma_h^2)\). The proof proceeds through four lemmas — one-step descent, client drift, aggregation error, and telescoping — establishing Theorem 1, which recovers classical FedAvg convergence exactly when \(\beta \to 0\). From the bound we derived actionable design rules: auxiliary networks must satisfy \(\beta = O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})\) for \(\varepsilon\)-accuracy, and improving auxiliary quality yields no benefit once \(\beta^2\) falls below the heterogeneity floor \(\sigma_h^2\).

We validated the theory on two experimental settings. Controlled MNIST experiments confirm each component of the bound independently: log-log slope \(\approx -0.5\) validates the \(O(1/\sqrt{T})\) rate; a unit slope between final error and \(\hat{\beta}^2\) validates the auxiliary quality floor; and the performance gap between weak and strong auxiliary networks vanishes exactly where \(\sigma_h^2 \approx \hat{\beta}^2\), confirming Corollary 5.2. Experiment 4 applies the method to the UCI Heart Disease dataset — four real hospital institutions across three countries — demonstrating that BP-free federated learning achieves competitive clinical accuracy without any institution exposing patient records or gradient information. The measured convergence floor closely matches the theoretical prediction \(\sqrt{\hat{\beta}^2 + \sigma_h^2}\), providing the first empirical validation of Theorem 1 on genuine medical data.

Beyond convergence, the BP-free federated framework offers a structural privacy advantage absent in standard FedAvg: no gradient signal crosses the client-server boundary, eliminating the gradient inversion attack surface entirely. Future work includes extending the analysis to partial client participation and asynchronous rounds, characterizing optimal auxiliary network architecture as a function of \(\varepsilon\) and \(\sigma_h\), and scaling validation to large clinical databases such as MIMIC-IV where heterogeneity across ICU populations is substantial and privacy constraints are legally binding.
